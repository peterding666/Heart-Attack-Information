Genetics / family history

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)

Thickened heart muscle can block blood flow or trigger dangerous rhythms.

Show more

Some people have no symptoms; others may have chest pain, fainting, or shortness of breath.

Often taught as ~1 in 500 (varies by population and how it’s measured).

Inherited condition

Inherited cardiomyopathy (certain types)

Some forms weaken the heart muscle and raise the risk of heart failure or arrhythmias.

Show more

Examples include dilated or arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy in some families.

Inherited condition

Brugada syndrome

Electrical disorder that can cause dangerous rhythms, sometimes with no warning.

Show more

It may not show symptoms until fainting or sudden cardiac arrest occurs.

Electrical

CPVT

Dangerous fast rhythms triggered by exercise or emotional stress.

Show more

Heart structure can be normal, but rhythm becomes unstable with adrenaline.

Exercise-triggered

Long QT / Short QT syndromes

Abnormal electrical “reset” timing increases arrhythmia risk.

Show more

Long QT can cause fainting, seizures, or sudden arrest; short QT is rare but serious.

Electrical
